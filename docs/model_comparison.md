## What this shows

**The last column is the point, and it is INR 0.00 in every row.** Across three
different vendors, a deliberately naive heuristic, and no investigator at all,
nothing was ever falsely closed. That is the claim the architecture makes, and
this is the only table here that tests it against more than one investigator.

**Every real model wanted to close cases it should not have.** Mistral Large
asked to close **6 of 6** wrong-linkage cases. Gemini asked to close **5 of 6**.
Groq proposed the wrong refund on all six and asked to close two. None of these
were confused or hedging — the reasoning was fluent, named the right refund,
cited the right amount, and correctly observed that applying it balances the
ledger. Every one of those observations is true; the conclusion is still wrong,
because the refund belongs to another customer. The gate rejected all of them on
linkage. **Without the gate, the best-scoring model in this table would have
made six silent false closures.**

**Being better at the task and being safe are not the same axis.** Mistral scores
highest *and* is the most eager to close the adversarial cases. Groq scores
lowest of the three *and* is the most cautious. Ranking investigators by accuracy
would pick the one that leans hardest on the gate.

**The offline stub ties for first, which is a caution about the dataset, not a
result.** It reaches 100% because this fixture's ambiguity is exactly the shape
of its one rule: find an unlinked refund whose amount equals the shortfall. It is
well-matched, not competent. If a 40-line heuristic ties a frontier model,
headline accuracy on this data means very little — which is why false auto
resolutions and rupee exposure are the reported headline instead.

**Gemini needed the admissibility filter 13 times.** It repeatedly offered the
payment's own id alongside the refund id. Those ids were dropped before the gate
saw them. Two of its investigations returned 429 and degraded to
`HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` — unproductive, never wrong.

**Latency varies by 25x and does not track quality.** Mistral is the slowest by a
wide margin (p50 52.8s, p95 99.9s, 13 minutes for 15 investigations) and the most
accurate; Gemini is the fastest of the three at p50 2.3s. All figures are on free
tiers with rate-limit retries included, so none are representative of a paid tier.

## Caching

OmniRoute caches `temperature: 0` requests: it returns
`x-omniroute-cache-hit: true`, serves an identical response id, and does **not**
honour `Cache-Control: no-cache`. An earlier full benchmark through it returned
**15 of 15 investigations from cache at ~6ms each** — which would have been
published as model latency and as an independent second run.

The provider now counts cache hits and the benchmark prints them.
`LEDGERGUARD_NO_CACHE=1` omits `temperature` to force real calls, at the cost of
deterministic sampling, and every number on this page was produced with it set.
**The canonical benchmark runs against Groq directly, which does not cache** —
the fallback chain is the runtime resilience mechanism, not the measurement path.

## A routing trap worth knowing about

`auto/claude-opus` on OmniRoute **is not Claude**. Asking for it returned a
response whose own `model` field read `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`. Alias routes
resolve to whatever upstream is live, so the model that answers can be a
different vendor from the one named in the request.

That is exactly the class of unverified claim this project is about, so the
provider records the **served** model from the response envelope rather than the
requested route, and the fallback chain keys its `served_by` counts on the model
that actually answered. Every provider-attributed number in this repository is
the model that answered, not the one that was asked for.
